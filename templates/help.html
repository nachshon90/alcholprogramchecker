Help

What this tool does

You type in your company and product details and add a picture of the label. The tool reads the words on the picture and compares them with what you typed, one item at a time. It then tells you which items match and which do not.

What it checks

  • Brand name – is it on the label, and does it match?
  • Class or type – for example Bourbon Whiskey, India Pale Ale.
  • Alcohol content – compared allowing the small difference the law permits. This differs by drink: 0.3 percentage points for beer, 0.15 for spirits, and 1.0 or 1.5 for wine depending on strength.
  • Net contents – how much is in the container, and whether that is a standard container size.
  • Bottler, producer or importer name and address.
  • Country of origin – for imported products.
  • Sulfite statement – wine must say CONTAINS SULFITES when it holds 10 parts per million or more.
  • The Government Health Warning – see below.

The Government Health Warning

Every alcoholic drink of 0.5% alcohol or more must carry this exact wording:

GOVERNMENT WARNING: (1) According to the Surgeon General, women should not drink alcoholic beverages during pregnancy because of the risk of birth defects. (2) Consumption of alcoholic beverages impairs your ability to drive a car or operate machinery, and may cause health problems.

The tool checks four separate things:

  1. Is it there, and is every part of the wording correct?
  2. Are the words GOVERNMENT WARNING in capital letters? This is required by law.
  3. Are the letters big enough? The law sets a minimum height: 1 mm on containers of 237 mL or less, 2 mm on containers up to 3 L, and 3 mm above that.
  4. Is the text spread out enough? No more than 12 characters may be squeezed into one inch.

To check letter height, the tool must know the real size of your label. Measure the printed label with a ruler and type its width in millimetres into the box on the check page. Without it, the tool will say it cannot check the letter height rather than guess.

About capital letters

Federal law requires only the words GOVERNMENT WARNING to be in capitals and bold. If the rest of the warning is in small letters, the tool marks that as ADVICE ONLY, because it is a house-style preference rather than a legal problem. Items marked ADVICE ONLY never cause a label to be reported as failing.

Why does it say “cannot check”?

The tool says this rather than guessing when it does not have what it needs – for example when the label picture is too blurry to read, or when you have not given the real label width. A guess on a safety rule would be worse than an honest answer.

Getting a good picture

  • Take the picture straight on, not at an angle.
  • Use good, even light and avoid glare or shadows.
  • Fill the frame with the label.
  • Make sure the small print is in focus.

Your privacy

This tool runs entirely on this computer. Pictures and company details are never sent over the internet, are not saved to any file or database, and are gone as soon as the page is finished with them.

One more thing

This tool is a checking aid. It is not an official government decision, and it does not replace review by a qualified person. Always confirm anything it flags by eye.
